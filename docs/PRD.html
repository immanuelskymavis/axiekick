§ 01

Why this game

Divekick strips a fighting game down to the single decision every fighting game is actually about: when do I commit to the air? Two buttons, one-hit rounds, and a match that resolves in under two minutes. That is exactly the shape of a game a small team can finish in a weekend and a room full of colleagues can learn in ten seconds.

The Axies bring the other half. Starter Axies are potatoes — no arms, no legs, no anthropomorphic silhouette to animate. That is a constraint, not a problem: an Axie is its body parts, and this game turns those parts into the weapon. Buba does not kick, he drills with his horn. Puffy does not kick, she inflates and cannonballs. The thing that reads as "which Axie is this" at 60 fps is the same thing that reads as "what is this matchup".

One-line pitch: Olek, Buba and Puffy fight over who gets to be the protagonist of the Origins tutorial. Best of five. First one to touch the other wins the round.

§ 02

Goals, non-goals, and how we know it worked

Goals

  • A game that is fun in the first 20 seconds, with no tutorial.
  • Three Axies who feel genuinely different to fight and to fight against.
  • Deterministic simulation from day one, so networking is a port and not a rewrite.
  • Anyone in the office can open a URL and be in a match in under 30 seconds.

Non-goals for v1

  • Meter, specials, Kick Factor, gems — all cut. The stripped core is the product.
  • Headshot slowdown, dizzy states, or any second hit state.
  • Wallet, NFT ownership, gene-accurate Axies, or any on-chain hook.
  • Progression, unlocks, matchmaking service, accounts.

§ 03

Rules of play

Two buttons. That is the whole input space, and every rule below exists to make those two buttons carry a full match.

Universal ruleset. All timings at a fixed 60 Hz simulation tick.
RuleBehaviourValue
DiveJump. Direction is always toward the opponent; there is no left/right input.per-Axie
Kick (airborne)Commit to the divekick. Angle and speed are locked at the moment of press — no steering.per-Axie
Kick (grounded)Kickback: a short backward hop. Your only retreat, and it leaves you grounded and vulnerable on landing.per-Axie
Both buttonsNothing. Reserved for v2 specials so muscle memory survives the update.—
Round winAny contact between your active hitbox and their hurtbox. One hit, round over.1 hit
MatchFirst to five round wins.FT5
Round timerShort enough that stalling is not a strategy.20 s
Hold the LineAt 5 s remaining a centre line appears; when time expires, whoever is on their own side of it wins. Both on the same side: the invader loses.t ≤ 5 s
TradeIf both hitboxes connect on the same tick, both players lose the round. Score is unchanged and the round restarts.double KO
Round resetKO freeze, then reset to opening positions.45 f + 60 f

There is no walking, no blocking, no health bar, and no crouch. A player who never presses a button loses to a player who presses one, and a player who mashes loses to a player who waits — that tension is the entire game and we protect it by adding nothing else.

Both players share one keyboard by default, which is the point — this is a game you play next to someone. Gamepads are auto-assigned to the first free player slot on their first button press. Key rebinding is a settings screen we do not build.

§ 06

Screens and match flow

  1. Title. Two lines: the logo and "press any button". No menu.
  2. Character select. Three portraits side by side. Dive moves the cursor, Kick locks in. Both players pick on the same screen; mirror matches allowed. Auto-advances 60 f after the second lock.
  3. Match. Round counter as five pips per side, timer centred, Hold-the-Line marker at 5 s. Names and class colour under each pip row. Nothing else on screen.
  4. Round end. Freeze on the connecting frame, class-coloured impact flash, winner's pip fills, reset.
  5. Match end. Winner card, then a rematch prompt: Kick to rematch with the same picks, Dive to return to select. The rematch path must be one button, because on demo day the queue is the bottleneck.

UI language follows the Origins battle kit: heavy angular frames, class-tinted accents, uppercase display type. We are borrowing the vocabulary, not rebuilding the HUD.

§ 08

Multiplayer

Online P2P is a headline feature of this game and it is not in the v1 weekend build. What v1 owes v2 is an engine that makes it a two-day job rather than a rewrite.

v1 — shipped this weekend

  • Local versus, shared keyboard or two gamepads.
  • Simulation is a pure function: step(state, {p1,p2}) → state.
  • Fixed 60 Hz tick, fixed-point or integer math only. No Math.random, no Date.now, no float accumulation in the sim.
  • Inputs are a 2-bit mask per player per tick, recorded to a ring buffer.
  • Rendering reads state, never writes it.

v2 — the fast follow

  • WebRTC DataChannel, unreliable + unordered, with a room code the host reads out loud.
  • Signalling: one tiny broker, or manual SDP paste for the first test.
  • Start on 3-frame input delay lockstep; it is honest on office LAN.
  • Rollback afterwards if the game survives the jam — the input ring buffer and pure step function are the only two prerequisites, and both ship in v1.
  • Desync canary: hash the state every 30 ticks and compare.

If day 3 comes in ahead of schedule, the correct use of the spare time is delay-based lockstep over WebRTC, not more characters.

§ 09

Technical shape

  • Stack: TypeScript, Vite, Canvas 2D. PixiJS only if the web-vfx overlay comes along, and if so it stays a separate layer above the game canvas.
  • Loop: accumulator-driven fixed timestep at 60 Hz with a render interpolation pass; spiral-of-death clamp at 5 ticks per frame.
  • Collision: circle-vs-circle for hurtboxes, swept segment for the active kick hitbox so nothing tunnels at 14 u/f.
  • Data: one characters.ts table, hot-editable in-build via the F2 overlay, exportable as JSON so tuning survives a reload.
  • Assets: one atlas per Axie plus one shared UI atlas. Total budget under 6 MB so the URL opens fast on office wifi.
  • Deploy: static build, internal host, no backend in v1.
§ 10

Three days

Day 1 — the feel

  • Fixed-timestep loop, deterministic step function, input ring buffer.
  • Capsules and debug hitboxes. No art.
  • One character, tuned until a whiffed dive feels genuinely bad.
  • Full round + match state machine, including Hold the Line and trades.
  • Gate: two people play FT5 with capsules and want a rematch.

Day 2 — the Axies

  • Export Olek, Buba, Puffy poses out of gtk2d into atlases.
  • Three movesets in the data table; first balance pass.
  • Character select, HUD, stage, impact VFX, core SFX.
  • Gate: a stranger can tell the three apart without reading the names.

Day 3 — the room

  • Gamepad support, one-button rematch, big-screen scaling.
  • Second balance pass with actual playtesters, not the team.
  • Deploy the URL, then playtest from the URL — not from localhost.
  • Spare capacity goes to WebRTC lockstep. Nothing else.
