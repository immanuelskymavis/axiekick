§ 01

Why a run mode

Versus is two people arguing. The CPU ladder is one person practising. Neither gives a single player a reason to keep going after the match ends, and at a demo station most people arrive alone.

Arcade gives the same two buttons a spine: ten fights, three decisions, and a reason to care about the third one. It also solves the demo-floor problem — a run is a story someone can watch over your shoulder, and dying on level 8 makes people want the machine.

The pitch in one line: ten Axies, three things you find, and by level ten they are carrying your build back at you.

§ 02

The run

Ten levels. Format escalates, the CPU sharpens, and from level 4 the opponent is drafting too. Tuned against a sparring bot with a human-shaped reaction delay: median run ends at stage 5, one in six clears.

Level 10 is the run's punchline. The final opponent is the Axie you beat on level 1, carrying the exact three augments you drafted. Whatever you decided was strong, you now have to beat. It costs almost nothing to build — the augment system already applies to either fighter — and it is the only boss that is different every run.

Lives

  • Three per run, shown as Origins star badges in the corner.
  • Losing a fight costs a life and you replay that level; the opponent's augments are re-rolled, so it is not the same fight twice.
  • Third loss ends the run on a summary card: level reached, augments taken, rounds won.

Pacing

  • Levels 1–3 are single rounds: about 40 seconds each, so a run gets going before anyone can lose interest.
  • Levels 4–7 are first to 3, 7–10 first to 5. A clean run is about 12 minutes; a bad one ends sooner, which is the point.
  • No menu between fights except the three draft screens.
§ 03

Augments

Three choices in a run, each one of three cards drawn from a pool of fifteen. Every augment is a Land item from the Origins asset kit, and the item is the thing you picked up — the sprite, the name and the line of lore all belong to it.

Two thirds of the pool bends a rule rather than nudging a number, because a run you can describe afterwards ("the one where I had wings and a shield") is worth more than one you can only measure.

The one that needs watching: Ward of the Ruins turns a one-hit game into a two-hit game for a fight. That is a bigger change than anything else in the pool and it is deliberately legendary and late — but if it makes level 10 trivial, it becomes "survive the first hit of the run" instead.

Draft rules: three cards, no rerolls, no skipping. Duplicates are removed from the pool once taken. At level 9 the pool is legendary-weighted so the last pick feels like a decision about how you want to win.

§ 04

What the player sees

  1. The ladder. Ten Origins map nodes across the screen — cleared ones carry the green trophy, the current one the orange crossed swords, losses the red flag. Your three augment medallions sit under it in avatar rings, and your lives are star badges. This is the only screen between fights.
  2. The fight. Exactly the game that already exists, plus a small augment strip under each name plate so both fighters can see what the other is carrying.
  3. The draft. Three item cards, big art, name, effect line, lore line. DIVE moves, KICK takes. No timer — this is the one moment in the game where thinking is allowed.
  4. The end card. Level reached, the three items you took, rounds won and lost, and the one stat worth bragging about: how many rounds your Reflection took off you.

All of it uses the interface pieces already packed from the kit — the node icons, the avatar ring, the star badge — so Arcade looks like Origins rather than like a menu bolted onto a jam game.

§ 05

How it fits the code

Arcade should not become a second game. It is a wrapper around the match that already exists.

  • Augments are a modifier table applied at match creation, not special cases in the simulation. Each one is a function from a character's stat block to a modified copy; the sim never learns what an augment is. Two exceptions earn their keep: Ward (one absorbed hit) and Ironroot (trade scoring), which need a flag each in the round-end path.
  • The CPU already reads stats rather than constants, so an opponent with augments is handled by the existing code — its rangeMax and kick solution move with the modified numbers.
  • Run state is a plain object: level, lives, the three augment ids, and an RNG seed. That makes a run replayable and, later, shareable as a seed.
  • Nothing here touches the netcode. Arcade is single-player; the online path is untouched.

§ 07

Not in this

  • No shop, no currency, no inventory. Three choices is the economy.
  • No unlocks between runs. Every run starts from the same place; the only progression is the player's.
  • No online Arcade, no leaderboard. A seed on the end card is the version of that worth building later.
  • No new Axies. The roster is the ladder.
  • No per-character augments. The pool is shared, and finding out that Wings on Momo means three jumps is the good kind of discovery.
